Semiconductor Layout Design Protection

Safeguard the three‑dimensional topography of integrated circuits.

What are Layout Designs (Topographies)?

Integrated circuits, or “chips,” are products in which electronic components and their interconnections are formed in a thin semiconductor material. A layout design (topography) is the three‑dimensional disposition of those elements and interconnections within an integrated circuit. These topographies are also called mask works or chip layouts.

Eligibility & Registration

A layout design can be protected if it is original—that is, the result of the creator’s own intellectual effort and not commonplace at the time of creation. In many jurisdictions, protection requires registration or first commercial exploitation. Registration generally does not involve substantive examination and must be filed within a certain time (often two years) after first commercial exploitation.

An application typically includes the owner’s details, a title and drawings or descriptions of the topography. In some countries, a sample of the integrated circuit and a description of its electronic function must also be provided.

Rights & Term of Protection

The right holder has the exclusive right to prevent others from reproducing the protected layout design, importing, selling or distributing integrated circuits or articles incorporating the layout without authorisation. According to the TRIPS Agreement, the minimum term of protection is ten years from the filing date or from the first commercial exploitation. Infringement can be challenged through civil actions; some countries also provide criminal remedies.

The protection is limited: it does not stop others from creating independent, original topographies that serve the same function. Activities such as private use, evaluation or reverse engineering for teaching and research generally do not require the right holder’s authorisation.

International Framework

Layout designs of integrated circuits are protected under sui generis laws, rather than traditional patent or copyright regimes. The Washington Treaty on Intellectual Property in Respect of Integrated Circuits (IPIC) provides definitions and basic requirements but has not entered into force; its provisions are incorporated into the TRIPS Agreement. Each country determines whether registration is required and which law applies; some treat layout designs under copyright, design or unfair competition laws. There is no international filing system similar to the PCT for layout designs—protection must be sought separately in each jurisdiction.
