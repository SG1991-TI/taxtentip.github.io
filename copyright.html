Copyright & Creative Works

Protecting original expression in literature, art, software and more.

What is Copyright?

Copyright protects original works of authorship once they are fixed in a tangible medium. A work is original when it is independently created by a human author and possesses a minimal degree of creativity; copyright protects the expression of an idea, not the idea itself【354090278546460†L110-L129】. To be protected, a work must be fixed in a sufficiently permanent medium such that it can be perceived or reproduced【354090278546460†L132-L137】.

Exclusive Rights

The copyright owner has the exclusive right to carry out or authorise the following activities【354090278546460†L168-L181】:

  • Reproduction: Making copies of the work.
  • Derivative works: Preparing adaptations or derivative works based on the original.
  • Distribution: Selling, leasing or otherwise distributing copies to the public.
  • Public performance: Performing the work publicly (e.g., music, plays, films).
  • Public display: Showing the work publicly (e.g., artwork, slides).

These rights allow creators to control how their work is used and monetised. Copyright does not protect facts, ideas, procedures or methods of operation; only the author’s original expression is protected【354090278546460†L110-L129】.

Fixation and Duration

Copyright arises automatically once a work is fixed. In most jurisdictions, the term lasts for the life of the author plus a certain number of years, although specific rules vary by country and type of work.
