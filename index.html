2026 IP Trends & Insights

The intellectual property landscape is rapidly evolving. In 2025 courts clarified key principles across trade marks, copyright, patents and designs while laying the groundwork for more significant developments【188866426508710†L172-L181】. Looking ahead to 2026, IP protection is no longer just about registering patents and trade marks; it’s about defending innovation in a digital world where AI and online threats make infringement easier to scale【181230335740269†L111-L125】.

  • Web security and IP converge: As more valuable assets live online, cyber‑attacks increasingly target source code, product documentation and proprietary datasets. EU regulations like NIS2 and DORA require organisations to embed cyber resilience into core operations【181230335740269†L120-L137】.
  • Continuous monitoring: With expanding cloud and identity‑based attack surfaces, organisations are adopting continuous exposure management; Gartner predicts those adopting CEM will be three times less likely to suffer a breach by 2026【181230335740269†L146-L151】.
  • Domain strategy as security: Domains have become a common entry point for phishing and brand abuse. The global domain base reached 386.9 million registrations in Q4 2025, so monitoring new registrations and lookalike patterns is becoming standard practice【181230335740269†L160-L170】.
  • Automation in brand protection: Counterfeit imports are estimated around $467 billion; successful brand‑protection programmes rely on continuous monitoring and rapid takedown capability【181230335740269†L190-L199】.
  • Strategic IP legal services: Investment in intangible assets has grown three times faster than investment in tangible assets since 2008【181230335740269†L234-L237】. Companies are shifting from broad, across‑the‑board filings to more targeted strategies and tackling new issues such as AI‑generated content ownership, copyright exposure from training datasets and patentability of AI‑assisted inventions【181230335740269†L248-L266】.
  • Notable cases & policy developments: Decisions such as Iconix v Dream Pairs show that post‑sale confusion can amount to trade‑mark infringement【188866426508710†L189-L200】. The UK government’s consultation on AI and copyright is expected to deliver outcomes in 2026, signalling further changes ahead【188866426508710†L264-L274】.
